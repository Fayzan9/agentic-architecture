# Stop Loss Gap Review (SLGR) — Business Requirements

**Checklist data:** `data/stop_loss_gap_review.json` (gitignored, local only) — 4 sections, 50 topics.

## What this assessment is

A **Stop Loss Gap Review (SLGR)** is a standalone, dedicated comparison between a **Plan Document** and a **Stop Loss Policy** (plus optional **Amendments** to the Plan and **Endorsements** to the Policy — later language is always controlling over the base document). Its purpose is narrower and deeper than PDASL's compliance-style checklist: for every topic, find the actual language in *both* documents and classify the **financial risk** to the employer if a gap exists between them.

**Input documents:** Plan Document (+ optional Amendments), Stop Loss Policy (+ optional Endorsements).

## The core business rule: the reimbursement-impact test

Every gap classification in SLGR answers exactly one question:

> **"Would the Stop Loss Policy's language, AS WRITTEN, cause a claim the Plan is obligated to pay to be denied, capped, or reimbursed at less than 100% by the stop-loss carrier?"**

Any unreimbursed amount falls on the employer — that financial exposure is the entire reason this assessment exists. Every topic is classified into exactly one of four tiers:

| Tier | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard Gap** | The Policy would, as written, cause a Plan-paid claim to be denied, capped, or partially reimbursed — a concrete, quantifiable financial exposure. Triggers: the Policy excludes/caps something the Plan covers without limit; the Policy defines a controlling term (Medically Necessary, Experimental/Investigational, Usual & Customary) more narrowly than the Plan; the Policy adds a condition/deadline/documentation requirement the Plan doesn't have and failing it forfeits reimbursement; the Policy directly contradicts the Plan's payment. | Plan covers a drug with no cap; Policy caps it at $250,000 — a $400,000 claim leaves $150,000 unreimbursed. |
| **Soft Gap** | A genuine scope/definitional/interpretive mismatch exists, but it would **not**, as written, by itself cause a denial or reduced reimbursement. The risk is interpretive, not mechanical — vague/differing wording, Policy silence deferring to the Plan, or a mismatch with no current dollar impact that could be disputed later. | Plan and Policy both cover "medically necessary" services but word the definition slightly differently; no exclusion or cap results. |
| **No Gap** | The Policy's treatment of the topic is aligned with the Plan (equivalent or broader) — no exclusion, limit, condition, or definitional mismatch that could reduce reimbursement. | Plan excludes experimental treatment; Policy excludes it too, using an equivalent or broader definition. |
| **Not Sure** | Language on one or both sides is missing, incomplete, or too ambiguous to apply the test with confidence — routes to human review rather than guessing. | — |

**Grounding rule:** the language quoted from each document must be **verbatim** — never paraphrased or invented. If the decisive language isn't actually present, the classification must be "Not Sure," not a guess. This is enforced by a two-pass reviewer→critic process where the critic independently re-verifies every quote and re-applies the reimbursement-impact test before the finding is finalized.

**Reporting rule:** the HARD GAP / SOFT GAP label is applied by the surrounding system from the structured classification field — it is deliberately never embedded as freeform text inside the written suggestion, so there is exactly one source of truth for the label rather than two that could drift apart.

## Special case: Policy-only topics (Miscellaneous Provisions)

Some **Miscellaneous Provisions** topics (e.g. Arbitration, Grace Period, Termination, Notice of Appeal) are evaluated **on the Policy alone**, with no Plan-side comparison — the question there isn't "does this create a gap vs. the Plan" but "does this Policy language itself create financial or operational risk for the employer." The output still names the risk, cites the exact Policy language, and recommends an action, but without a hard/soft/no-gap tier (there's nothing on the Plan side to compare against).

## Special case: Policies with sparse boilerplate definitions/exclusions

The business logic includes a **canned observation** for when a Stop Loss Policy is unusually thin on its own definitions/exclusions (e.g. no explicit "usual and customary," "experimental," or "medical necessity" language) and instead defers to the Plan's own wording: this is flagged as *encouraging but not a guarantee* — even identical wording between the two documents can still be interpreted differently by the carrier, so deferral reduces but does not eliminate soft-gap risk.

## Notes

- Unlike PDA/PDASL, SLGR topics carry almost no `why_this_is_important` text — instead the business rationale for each topic lives in its `sample_suggestions` field, which doubles as a **worked example** of the kind of finding the topic tends to produce (a real past write-up, used to calibrate tone/depth, not a fact about the specific plan under review). Full worked examples for every topic live in the source JSON.
- SLGR no longer uses retrieval/chunking — both the full Plan text and full Policy text are given directly for every topic; `search_terms_plan` / `search_terms_stop_loss` remain in the schema for compatibility but aren't operative business logic.
